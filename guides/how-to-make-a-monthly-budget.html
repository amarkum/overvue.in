How to make a monthly budget you'll actually stick to

Most budgets fail because they're too detailed to keep up with. A simple one you check every few days beats a perfect one you abandon in week two.

2 min read · Updated 3 October 2026

1. Start from what you really spend

Before setting a limit, look at the last one to three months. Add up what went out on everyday spending: food, transport, shopping, bills, entertainment. This is your honest baseline. A budget set below reality with no plan for how to get there is a wish, not a budget.

2. Take out the fixed costs

Rent, loan EMIs, insurance and subscriptions are mostly fixed each month. Note them, and make sure your income covers them plus your savings goal before anything else.

3. Set one monthly spending limit

Instead of a dozen category envelopes, start with a single number for everything you spend in a month. It's far easier to keep track of, and it still tells you the one thing you need to know: are you on track or not? You can split it into categories later if one area keeps running away.

4. Turn it into a daily pace

Divide what's left by the days left in the month. If you have ₹17,500 left with 11 days to go, your pace is about ₹1,590 a day. That number is much easier to act on than a monthly total: it tells you whether today's dinner out fits.

5. Check in, don't wait for the month to end

Glance at your budget every few days. If your spending so far, continued at the same rate, would take you over by month-end, you'll know with time to change course. Waiting for the statement means finding out after the money is gone.

6. Adjust honestly

At the end of each month, compare the limit with what you spent. If you're always over, either the limit is unrealistic or one category needs attention. Change one thing at a time.

Tips that help

  • Log spending the same day. Small purchases are the ones you forget.
  • Pay yourself first: move savings out on payday, then budget what's left.
  • Plan for irregular costs such as birthdays, trips and annual fees with a small amount set aside each month.
